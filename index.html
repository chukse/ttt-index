The numbers people argue about

The TTT Index is a set of proprietary, transparent football ratings, the kind of number people cite and argue about. Where PFF's grades sit behind a paywall and a black box, ours are public and fully decomposable: every rating breaks into the factors that built it, and you can sort and challenge each one. It's the data standard for the overlooked question in pro football, not what happened, but who's actually good.

QBIRThe Quarterback Independent Rating

Every QB graded as if dropped into a neutral situation, opponent, scheme and supporting cast partialled out by a play-level model, then fused with PFF grades, production and track record into one 0–100 number. Seven visible factors, a value-over-average (PTS/G), and a turnover-luck column.

Transparent, situation-stripped, honest

Anyone can tell you what a player or team did. We estimate how good they actually are, and show every step.

01Situation-independent

A play-level ridge model partials out the opponent, the scheme and the supporting cast. QBIR asks what the quarterback himself is worth, regardless of where he plays. The Coach Index credits each scheme to the coach who ran it.

02Fully decomposable

No black box. Every grade breaks into its factors, every factor carries its league rank, and you can sort by any of them. PFF won't show you why a number is what it is. We will.

03Honest about limits

Small samples carry a reliability flag. Data gaps are stated, not faked. When a number leans on a prior or is early-season, the page says so. The limits are part of the story.

04Built on real data

nflverse play-by-play and charting, PFF grades, and our own models, fused into one rating. The inputs are nflverse and PFF; the synthesis, the attribution and the number are ours.
