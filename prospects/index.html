How the board is built

Most prospect boards grade on tape and brand name. This one asks a narrower, testable question: what does a college player's production actually tell you about the NFL? Every player's college production is mapped against the realized NFL careers of every prospect who came before him — no tape grades, no brand names, no insider buzz, just what the production can and can't predict.

We fixed the flaw that quietly flatters every study like this: survivorship. Instead of looking only at college players who reached the NFL, we keep the whole prospect pool — the busts included — and ask first whether a player even becomes a pro. Then we cross-validate: the model is scored on players it never trained on, so these numbers are honest out-of-sample, not a curve fit to the past.

The headline finding is a humbling one. You cannot predict how many NFL yards a player will gain — that's mostly draft capital, landing spot and luck. So we never print a yardage projection. What does hold up is the ranking of who's more likely to hit, and a calibrated probability of becoming a real contributor — and even the #1 player in the country is often a coin flip. That honesty is the product.

Reading the numbers

  • SCOREProduction ceiling  correlation-weighted college production + strength of schedule, ranked. Validated as an order.
  • MAKE%Will he hit?  calibrated P(real NFL contributor). When it says 40%, ~40% of such profiles made it.
  • DRIVERWhy  which college stats carry his probability — click the row for the full breakdown.

Beyond production: each player's recruiting pedigree is folded in — the one scouting signal measurable without charting, and it sharpens every board, most of all on defense where box-score production is noisiest. Trust by position: strongest at receiver, then the secondary and linebackers; solid at QB, back and the defensive line; weakest at tight end, where the numbers can't see blocking, testing or late development. Each board states its out-of-sample AUC and base rate up top.
